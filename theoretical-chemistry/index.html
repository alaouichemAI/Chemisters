What it is

The field in one minute

Theoretical chemistry uses mathematics and physics to explain chemical behaviour, and often to predict it before any experiment is run.

Its central tool is quantum mechanics: describe the electrons correctly and you obtain bond lengths, energies, colours and reaction barriers. It builds the theories that computational chemistry turns into software.

1926Schrödinger publishes his wave equation
1929Dirac: the laws of chemistry are known; the equations are just too hard to solve exactly
1998Nobel Prize to Kohn and Pople for density functional theory and quantum chemistry methods

Molecular orbital theory

Electrons spread over the whole molecule in orbitals.

Explains benzene's stability and oxygen's magnetism.

Valence bond theory

Bonds as overlapping atomic orbitals, with resonance.

Pauling's hybridization explains carbon's tetrahedral shape.

Density functional theory

Computing the energy from the electron density instead of the wavefunction.

The workhorse method in materials and catalysis research.

Statistical mechanics

From molecular energy levels to temperature, pressure and entropy.

Partition functions predict equilibrium constants.

Reaction dynamics
