Requirements

All 20 are mandatory

We can't publish a post that is missing any of these items. If something is missing, we'll ask you for it before publishing.

The content

  1. 1

    Published, peer-reviewed work

    The article must already be published in a peer-reviewed journal. Preprints, theses and work under review are not accepted.

    Include all three: the full article title, the journal name, and the DOI link (https://doi.org/…).

  2. 2

    A hook in the first two lines

    LinkedIn shows only about two lines (around 200 characters) before “…see more”. Those lines decide whether anyone keeps reading.

    Open with the problem your work solves or your most striking result. Don't open with “I'm pleased to share…”.

    Good“Steel coatings fail the moment they're scratched. What if the coating could repair the damage itself?”
  3. 3

    Why it matters, in plain language

    One sentence explaining why the work matters, written so that a chemist outside your specialty, or a student, understands it.

    Define every abbreviation the first time you use it, e.g. “metal–organic frameworks (MOFs)”.

  4. 4

    3–5 key results, with numbers

    Present them as short bullet points. At least three of them must include a concrete number: a yield, an efficiency, a selectivity, a stability time, an energy, a comparison with a reference.

  5. 5

    Results, not methods

    Each highlight must say what you found, not only which technique you used.

    Avoid“EIS was used to evaluate the coatings.”Good“The coating kept 96.9% protection efficiency after 7 days in salt water.”
  6. 6

    The mechanism: how it works

    One or two sentences explaining how or why your result happens: the key interaction, step or design idea. Readers remember the “how” far better than the “what”.

  7. 7

    Why it matters for the page's field

    A short paragraph (2–4 sentences) linking your work to the topic of the page where it will appear, e.g. catalysis, electrochemistry or medicinal chemistry.

    If the link to that field is weak, we'll suggest a better-matching Chemisters page instead.

  8. 8

    A short outlook

    At most two sentences on what comes next: future work, open questions or applications. Longer outlooks lose readers.

  9. 9

    A closing question to the audience

    End with a specific question that invites comments. Comments are what spread a post further.

    Avoid“What do you think?”Good“Which matters more for the next generation of coatings: self-healing or thermal stability?”
  10. 10

    Target length: about 1,200–1,800 characters

    This is the length that performs best. Use short paragraphs of 1–3 lines with a blank line between them, so the post reads well on a phone.

  11. 11

    Hard limit: never more than 3,000 characters

    LinkedIn refuses any post longer than 3,000 characters. Everything counts: spaces, line breaks, emojis, bullet symbols, the article title, the DOI link, author names and hashtags.

    Check the total before sending, for example with the post checker on this page. Posts over the limit are sent back to you to shorten.

  12. 12

    Every point appears only once

    No repeated bullets, results or paragraphs. Each result is stated in one place, and the outlook appears in one section only. Repetition wastes characters and readers notice it.

    AvoidTwo bullets both saying “97.6% efficiency after 24 h”AvoidA “Future work” paragraph and a “What comes next?” section saying the same thing
  13. 13

    Accurate to the article

    Every number, claim and name must match the published article exactly. As the author, you're responsible for the accuracy of the content. Don't announce results that aren't in the paper.

What to send with it

  1. 14

    One image

    Your graphical abstract or a key figure from the article. Posts with an image reach a much wider audience.

    Send it as a PNG or JPG file, at least 1,200 pixels wide, with text large enough to read on a phone. You must have the right to share it: your own figure, or an open-access figure credited to the article.

  2. 15

    3–6 relevant hashtags

    Include the field of the page (e.g. #Catalysis) and your key material or method (e.g. #MOFs, #DFT). Avoid generic tags like #Research or #Science only, and don't exceed six.

  3. 16

    Your name exactly as it appears on LinkedIn

    Plus the link to your LinkedIn profile, so we tag the right person.

  4. 17

    Your co-authors' names

    Their full names and, if they're on LinkedIn, their profile links. We tag them too, so the post reaches each of their networks.

  5. 18

    The link to your own LinkedIn post about the article

    We place it in the first comment under the featured post, so readers can find your original post and your profile. Copy the link directly from LinkedIn (“Copy link to post”) rather than retyping it.

  6. 19

    Clean, final, plain text

    Proofread the text and remove leftover headings, empty bullet points, comments and notes from editing.

    Send it as plain text in the message, not only as a Word file. Bold, italics and other Word formatting are lost on LinkedIn. Typing “@Name” as text doesn't tag anyone, because we add the tags ourselves when publishing.

Revisions

  1. 20

    You apply all feedback yourself

    The Chemisters team never modifies your text: we don't edit, add, delete or shorten anything, not even a single word or a duplicated line. What you send is exactly what gets published.

    When your post doesn't meet a requirement, we send you feedback. You revise the post yourself and send back the complete corrected version, not only the changed parts.

    A post is published only once all feedback on the required items has been applied. Each new version is checked again against all 20 requirements.

Recommended

Optional extras that boost reach
  1. 1

    Be active in the first hour

    Like, comment on and reshare the post from your own profile soon after it's published, and reply to comments. Early engagement is what pushes a post to more feeds.

  2. 2

    Your institution and lab

    Tell us your university, institute or lab and their LinkedIn pages, so we can tag them and reach their followers.

  3. 3

    The human story

    One sentence on what surprised you or what motivated the work. Readers connect with people, not only with data.

  4. 4

    Open access

    If the article is free to read, say so (“open access”). Readers click far more on articles they can read without a subscription.

  5. 5

    A second visual

    A second figure, or a short video of 15–60 seconds explaining your work, makes the post stand out even more.

  6. 6

    Contact details

    Your email, if you'd like readers or potential collaborators to contact you directly.

  7. 7

    Image description

    One sentence describing your image (alt text), so readers using screen readers can follow it too.

  8. 8

    Other suitable pages

    If your work spans several fields, tell us which other Chemisters pages it could fit, with one line on why for each.

    How to submit

    1. Choose the Chemisters page that best matches your field. See all our pages.
    2. Send that page a LinkedIn message with your post text, your image, the link to your own LinkedIn post about the article, and your co-authors' names.
    3. We check the 20 requirements and send you feedback if anything is missing or needs to change.
    4. You apply the feedback and send back the complete corrected version.
    5. Once every requirement is met, we publish your post exactly as you sent it.

    Not sure which page fits your work? Open the table of all our pages and pick the closest field. If two fit, tell us in your message.
