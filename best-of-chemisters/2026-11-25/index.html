IR relaxation times assigned to contact motions

University of Freiburg · University of Zurich

We combined transient infrared spectroscopy with nonequilibrium molecular dynamics simulations to connect experimental relaxation times to local inter‑residue contact distances. Contact‑distance networks capture coordinated motions that mediate long‑range allosteric coupling, allowing each kinetic step to be assigned to a specific molecular motion.

“Local Molecular Motions Encode Time-Resolved Infrared Spectra of Proteins”

Quinazoline hydrogenation: 98% yield, 99% ee

Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Yangzhou University · Universität Münster · Nanjing University

A solo‑coordination‑vacant Ir(III) pincer catalyst enables catalytic asymmetric partial hydrogenation of quinazolines, delivering enantioenriched 3,4‑dihydroquinazolines. Product‑induced catalyst deactivation has limited quinazoline reductions; this robust Ir(III) pincer catalyst overcomes poisoning, allowing broad substrate scope, high yields, and ultra‑low loadings (10 ppm, TON 38 000).

“Asymmetric Partial Hydrogenation of Quinazolines: Overcoming Catalyst Poisoning by a Robust yet Active Solo-Coordination-Vacant Ir(III) Pincer Catalyst”

Hollow COF with H2PO3 achieves 0.5 ng L⁻¹ OCP detection

Fuzhou University · Wuyi University · Minjiang Teachers College

The study converts imine bonds into α‑aminophosphonic acid linkages via linkage hydrophosphonylation, forming hollow COF spheres with H2PO3 groups in a single step. The hollow structure increases surface area and shortens mass‑transfer pathways, while H2PO3 sites provide multiple non‑covalent interactions (van der Waals, π‑π, hydrogen‑bond, dipole, halogen), yielding rapid, high‑affinity OCP capture and sub‑ng L⁻¹ detection.

“Linkage Hydrophosphonylation-Mediated Fabrication of Hollow Phosphonic Acid-Functionalized Covalent Organic Frameworks for Highly Efficient Enrichment and Detection of Organochlorine Pesticides”

04Organic ChemistryThe Journal of Organic Chemistry Q1

Acridine photocatalyst forms 1,3,4-oxa(thia)diazoles

Universidad de Alicante

Acridine photocatalyst enables a two‑step, one‑pot synthesis of 1,3,4‑oxa(thia)diazoles from (thio)semicarbazides and aldehydes, and radical oxidation of the in‑situ hydrazone is supported by mechanistic studies. The protocol works without molecular oxygen, using DCE as both solvent and terminal oxidant, providing a flexible, oxygen‑independent route to heterocycles.

“Direct Photocatalytic Synthesis of 1,3,4-Oxa(thia)diazoles from Aldehydes and (Thio)hydrazides”

Mem‑OV gives 734 nm & 514 nm for ONOO− and viscosity

Central China Normal University

The probe Mem‑OV rapidly turns on fluorescence at 734 nm for viscosity and at 514 nm for ONOO−, with no channel crosstalk and strong membrane localization. Because Mem‑OV simultaneously visualizes viscosity and ONOO− at the membrane, it can track ferroptosis dynamics, differentiate tumor from normal cells, and be applied in vivo through injection.

Detects CTCs at 10 cells/mL; miR‑21 LOD 4.12×10⁻¹³ M

Xuzhou Medical University · Zhejiang Sci-Tech University · The Affiliated Xuzhou Municipal Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University

We built a vertical‑contact‑separation triboelectric nanogenerator that powers a biosensor incorporating aptamer‑or CRISPR‑based nanohydrogels, MWCNTs and NiPt nanoparticles for voltage‑modulated detection of circulating tumor biomarkers. Self‑powered operation removes external power needs, while voltage readout with a multimeter offers simple, quantitative analysis; the platform reaches 10 cells/mL CTC detection and 4.12×10⁻¹³ M miR‑21 LOD, supporting point‑of‑care cancer monitoring.

CRISPR/Cas12a assay detects mecA at 213 fM

Fuzhou University · Zhejiang University · Nanjing University of Finance and Economics · City University of Hong Kong · Shenzhen Research Institute of City University of Hong Kong · The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

We developed an amplification‑free, label‑free CRISPR/Cas12a biosensing platform that uses DNA‑templated copper nanoclusters (DNA‑CuNCs) as fluorescent reporters together with exonuclease III and a rationally designed AT‑rich DNA template. The method offers low‑cost, high signal‑to‑background fluorescence without amplification, enabling point‑of‑care testing on ice and accurate detection of antibiotic‑resistance genes in environmental samples.

“A Low-Cost Non-FRET Reporter System Empowers CRISPR/Cas12a Based Detection”
