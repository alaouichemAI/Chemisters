Electrolytes achieving <6% SEI loss and 99.98% CE

Today's selection highlights electrochemical advances: Na‑ion electrolytes with 6% SEI loss and 99.98% Coulombic efficiency, a B‑N tetraquinane reaching –3.2 V reduction potential, and a Na‑based coating retaining 80% capacity after 511 cycles at 4.7 V. 10 papers from Q1 journals, each with a link to the original. 01ElectrochemistryJournal of the American Chemical Society Q1

High‑EState electrolytes cut SEI loss to 6% & 99.98% CE

University of Cambridge · Nanjing University · China · UK

We measured SEI dissolution for many Na‑ion electrolytes, used a closed‑loop data‑driven workflow to link dissolution to >200 physicochemical descriptors, and identified the solvent average EState index as the key predictor. SEI dissolution depletes Na inventory, causing rapid capacity loss; minimizing dissolution with high‑EState electrolytes reduces Na loss and enables near‑unity Coulombic efficiency, extending battery life.

“Data-Driven Electrolyte Design by Electrotopological State Descriptors for Na-Ion Batteries with Minimal Solid-Electrolyte Interphase Dissolution”

02Organic ChemistryJournal of the American Chemical Society Q1

B,N tetraquinane reduces at –3.2 V, cleaves bonds

City University of Hong Kong · The Chinese University of Hong Kong

Synthesized a bowl‑shaped B–N tetraquinane in one high‑yield step and measured a reduction potential of −3.2 V vs Fc/Fc+, a record for organic reductants. Topological strain in the 3‑D bowl creates a highly destabilized boron dianion, giving exceptionally negative reduction potential and enabling reductive activation of strong C–Si, C–C, and C–F bonds.

“A Bowl-Shaped B,N-Tetraquinane Organic Super-Reductant”

03ElectrochemistryJournal of the American Chemical Society Q1

80% capacity retained after 511 cycles at 4.7 V

Qingdao University · ShanghaiTech University · Chinese Academy of Sciences · Tsinghua University

A Na⁺-based solid electrolyte coating (Na₃Zr₂Si₂PO₁₂) on NCM811 undergoes Li⁺/Na⁺ exchange, forming a fast Li⁺‑conducting L‑NZSP interphase and inserting Na⁺ into the cathode lattice. The L‑NZSP layer suppresses high‑voltage parasitic reactions, while the incorporated Na⁺ pillars stabilize the oxygen lattice, reducing chemo‑mechanical degradation and enabling long‑life high‑energy operation.

“Dynamic Li+/Na+-Exchanging Interphase Stabilizes High-Voltage Ni-Rich Cathodes for 557 Wh kg–1 Lithium Batteries”

78.9% FE glycine at 533 mA cm⁻², 1000 h operation

Chinese Academy of Sciences · Beijing National Laboratory for Molecular Sciences · Institute of Chemistry · University of Chinese Academy of Sciences · Yanshan University · State Key Laboratory of Metastable Materials Science and Technology · China

A Bi‑based nanoalloy with atomically dispersed Cu and Mn (BiCuMn@C) was synthesized and applied to glycine electrosynthesis. Cu induces d‑p hybridization with Bi, improving nitrogen species adsorption, while Mn supplies spin‑polarized electrons to *NO, enabling its hydrogenation to *NHO and breaking the *NO/*NHO scaling relationship, thus raising activity and selectivity.

“Spin and Charge Engineering of a BiCuMn Nanoalloy Catalyst for Industrial‐Scale Electrosynthesis of Glycine”

Colloid‑guided WBG films give 34% PCE in Si tandems

H speeds Pd dispersion on Pd‑Ag(100), slows it on (111)

Fudan University · Collaborative Innovation Center of Chemistry for Energy Materials · Shanghai Institute of Organic Chemistry · China

Large‑scale MD simulations resolved H‑driven surface reconstruction kinetics on Pd‑covered Ag, revealing Pd‑Ag exchange at domain edges and Ag encapsulation of Pd, with facet‑dependent rates. The facet‑dependent HIR creates Pd₃ triangular sites that enhance selective acetylene hydrogenation, demonstrating that hydrogen‑induced reconstruction governs active‑site structure and catalytic selectivity.

Dipolar interface gives 26.86% efficiency in rigid PSCs

Henan Normal University · King Abdullah University of Science and Technology · Tianjin University · Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications · China · Saudi Arabia

A zwitterionic molecule, Pyps, was placed at the SnO₂ ETL/perovskite interface to form a dipolar heterointerface that reinforces bonding and passivates defects, while its intrinsic dipole aligns energy levels for better charge extraction.
